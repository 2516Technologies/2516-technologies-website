The Keynote

When Survival Becomes Your Identity

The behaviors that save us can eventually limit us.
A practical, human examination of identity, adaptation, and leadership.

Most people believe trauma is something that happened. In reality, trauma is often something we continue doing.

Through stories from combat, recovery, entrepreneurship, and leadership, Mark challenges audiences to reconsider the difference between identity and adaptation—and to recognize unconscious patterns before they shape careers, relationships, and organizations.

The goal is not to eliminate the parts of us that learned to survive. It is to understand them, integrate them, and decide whether they should still be in charge.

  • Hypervigilance→Attention to detail
  • Control→Leadership
  • Emotional suppression→Professionalism
  • Relentless productivity→Ambition
  • Distrust→Skepticism
  • Self-sacrifice→Team player

About Mark

Experience from environments where decisions carry real consequences.

Mark Henning is an Army veteran, former combat medic, entrepreneur, technology consultant, and founder of 2516 Technologies.

His work brings together lessons from Afghanistan, trauma recovery, commercial analytics, regulated industries, and organizational leadership to explore a single question: what invisible systems are shaping the decisions we can see?

Mark does not ask audiences to discard the parts of themselves that learned to survive. He shows them how to recognize those parts, understand what they protected, and choose who leads next.
